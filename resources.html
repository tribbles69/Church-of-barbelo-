The Church of Barbelo

A Note on Sources

Most of what survives of the older Gnostic layer survives in the words of those who wrote against it. The early Church writers below were hostile witnesses: they described these traditions in order to refute them, and they were not always fair. They are still the oldest accounts we have, and we read them closely and with that in mind.

Everything in the first section is free to read online in public-domain translations.

IThe Ancient Witnesses

  • Irenaeus, Against Heresies I.29

    Written around 180 CE. The earliest description of the Barbelo-Gnostics, and of Barbelo as the first Thought.

  • Irenaeus, Against Heresies I.30

    The most substantial early account of the Ophite system: the serpent, the demiurge and the archons.

  • Hippolytus, Refutation of All Heresies, Book V

    Early third century. The Naassenes, who took their name from the Hebrew word for serpent, and related groups.

  • Origen, Against Celsus VI.24–38

    Around 248 CE. Origen’s description of the Ophite Diagram, a map of the heavens enclosed by the serpent Leviathan.

  • The Pistis Sophia, translated by G.R.S. Mead

    A Gnostic text preserved in Coptic, in Mead’s 1921 translation. Later than our layer, but rich in its cosmology.

IIThe Texts Themselves

  • The Nag Hammadi Library

    The codices found in Egypt in 1945, in English, hosted by the Gnostic Society. These translations are still in copyright, so we link to them rather than reproduce them.

  • Coptic Scriptorium

    Openly licensed Coptic texts, including the Gospel of Thomas, for anyone reading in the original language.

  • The Pleroma Archive

    Our companion library of restored texts and submitted writing, kept editorially independent of the Church.

IVGlossary

Monad
The Invisible Spirit: the source beyond everything, which cannot be named or described.
Barbelo
The first Thought of the Monad, its first emanation, and the first thing that can be named.
Barbeline
Our word for this tradition, taken from the ancient name Barbeliotae.
Pleroma
“The fullness”: the whole, undivided realm of light.
Aeon
One of the divine beings or powers within the Pleroma.
Kosmos
The material world as a whole, which this tradition treats as a prison for the spark.
Demiurge
“The craftsman”: the power who made the material world. Often named Ialdabaoth.
Archons
“Rulers”: the powers under the demiurge who hold the material order.
Sabaoth
The upper archon who, in our teaching, repented and now helps souls through the last archonic layer.
The spark
The portion of the fullness held in every person.
Gnosis
Knowledge of the spark: recognition, not belief.
Ophite
From the Greek ophis, serpent. A label the early Church writers gave to groups who honoured the serpent of Eden.
Heresiologist
An early Church writer who catalogued and refuted the movements they judged to be heresies.
