Questions

Short answers to what people usually ask first

The Church of Barbelo

Is the Church of Barbelo a Christian church?

Not in the usual sense. We are a Gnostic church, and we work from the oldest layer of the tradition: the cosmology as it stood before a Christian frame was set over it. Jesus is not the figure we work from. Our reasons are set out in What We Hold.

Who or what is Barbelo?

Barbelo is the first Thought of the Invisible Spirit: its first emanation, and the first thing that can be named. The Spirit itself is beyond any name, so the Church is named for her instead.

What does “Barbeline” mean?

It is our word for the tradition we follow. Ancient writers called our predecessors Barbeliotae. We take the root of that name and leave behind the hostile labels that came with it.

Do you worship snakes?

No. In our reading, the serpent in Eden brought knowledge to the first humans rather than ruin. We honour that reading, but we do not worship the serpent, and we decline the old label of “snake-worshippers”.

Do you think the body is evil?

No. We hold that the whole material world is the work of lesser powers, so the body is no worse than any other matter. It is a garment worn inside that world, and it need not be feared or hated.

Are LGBT people welcome?

Yes. We hold that the soul has no sex, and the Church blesses the union of any two souls who come to her in love, whatever bodies they wear. This is set out in On the Union of Souls.

Do I have to believe everything on this site?

No. Our teaching is offered as an opt-in path, and our page on what we hold marks clearly which parts are settled teaching and which are only proposals. You do not need to believe anything at all to write to us.

Is the Pleroma Archive part of the Church?

The Archive was founded by the Church, but it is run independently. It admits material on its merits, not on doctrinal grounds, and nothing in it carries the Church’s endorsement.

Who leads the Church?

The Church of Barbelo was founded by the Rt Revd Aaron Clews, its bishop.

How do I join?

There is nothing to sign. If something here has caught you, write to us in your own words through the enquiries page. A newsletter is coming soon for anyone who wants to follow the Church’s work.
