What We Hold

The teaching and practice of the Church of Barbelo

Draft for review
Issued from the office of the Bishop

Preamble

This page sets out what the Church of Barbelo teaches, as plainly as we can put it. Each section is marked with how firmly it is held: what the Church teaches, and what is offered only as a proposal for discussion. We would rather be clear about the difference than present a guess as a certainty.

IHow Our Teaching Is Held

Taught by the Church

Our practice is fixed. Our cosmology is held more loosely. The Gnostic paths have always differed in how they map the heavens, and we do not pretend that any one map is settled. What we teach as our orthodoxy is what the older, proto-Gnostic paths hold in common.

That orthodoxy is for those who choose to take it up. It is an opt-in path for members of this Church, set out so that anyone who wants structure can find it. It is not a measure we hold up against other Gnostic churches, and we do not ask them to adopt it.

IIThe Unnameable and the First Thought

Taught by the Church

Beyond everything is the Invisible Spirit, the Monad. It cannot be named or described, and any name given to it would be a name for something less than it.

Barbelo is the first thing that can be named: the Spirit's own thought of itself, the first emanation, from which the fullness unfolds. The Church takes her name for that reason. A tradition must be named for something a person can turn toward, and she is the first such thing.

IIIThe Fullness and the World

Taught by the Church

The Pleroma, the fullness, is the whole and undivided realm of light. The world we live in is not that realm.

All matter is the work of the archons, the powers who fashioned the material order. This is not a teaching that the body is uniquely corrupt. The prison is the whole kosmos: earth, sea, sky and stars. The body is a garment worn inside that prison, no worse than any other matter, and it need not be feared or hated.

IVThe Demiurge, the Archons and Sabaoth

Taught by the Church

The material order was made by the demiurge and the archons beneath him. They hold the world, but they do not hold the spark.

Among the archons, the Church gives a particular place to Sabaoth: the upper archon who repented and ascended, and who now helps souls to pass through the last archonic layer on their way out of the world.

The wider pantheon is still being settled from the Gnostic texts and the heresiologists' reports, and this section will grow as that work is done.

VThe Serpent

Taught by the Church

In our reading, the serpent in Eden brought knowledge rather than ruin. It is the revealer who opened the first humans' eyes to what had been kept from them. Our predecessors were catalogued as snake-worshippers on the strength of this. We keep the reading and decline the label.

VIThe Spark and Gnosis

Taught by the Church

Something of the fullness is held in each person. It is not earned, no institution can grant it, and no institution can take it away. Gnosis is knowledge of that spark: not belief about it, but recognition of it.

VIIChrist and the Older Layer

Taught by the Church

The Church works from the older layer of the tradition: the cosmology as it stood before a Christian frame was set over it. Jesus is not the figure we work from, for three reasons.

  • The story of Jesus as it has come down to us was reshaped by the consolidation of the early church and its politics, and we cannot recover it cleanly.
  • In that received story, Jesus identifies his Father with the God of the Hebrew scriptures, whom this tradition reads as the demiurge.
  • We hold that the Christ spirit has moved through many people in many ages. It did not enter the world once and depart.

VIIILove and Marriage

Taught by the Church

The soul has no sex. Sex belongs to the body, not to the spark. Marriage is therefore a union of souls, and the Church blesses the union of any two souls who come to her in love, without regard to the bodies they wear.

This teaching is set out in full in On the Union of Souls.

IXPractice

Taught by the Church

The Church keeps a practice of speaking aloud. It is modelled on confession, but its purpose is not absolution. Saying a thing aloud, to someone who will not judge it, makes it easier to understand and easier to forgive in yourself. No one else forgives you; the speaking is what helps.

The full practice of the Church, its rites and prayers, will be set out here as each part is finalised.

XA Proposal: The Demiurgic Egregore

A proposal, not a teaching

This section offers a new line of thought for discussion. It is not doctrine, and members are not asked to hold it.

An egregore is a presence that a group's shared belief brings into being. The proposal is that the demiurge may be such a presence: shaped by collective belief, and able to act across time, so that belief in him could, in effect, write him backwards into creation.

If that is so, gnosis starves the demiurge. As people come to know the spark and stop feeding him with belief, he is weakened, though he persists. We do not claim to know how far the reach of belief extends.

Given under the sign of Barbelo.
