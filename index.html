Welcome

A Barbeline church

We are a small religious body who hold that the human being carries a spark of the divine fullness, and that the purpose of a life is to know it.

We work from the oldest layer of the tradition: the cosmology as it stood before a Christian frame was set over it. That layer has no single saviour in it. The Christ spirit has moved through many, in many ages, and we do not hold that it entered the world once and departed. Jesus is not the figure we work from; he is simply not the layer we work from.

What survives of that layer survives mostly in the words of people who wrote to condemn it. Part of our work is ordinary religious life — teaching, rites, care for members. The other part is recovering the tradition from those hostile accounts and setting it out plainly, so that it can be judged on what it actually says.

You do not need to believe anything to write to us, and there is nothing to sign. If you have come here because something in the material has caught you, that is reason enough.

Teaching

Four things we hold

Stated briefly here. Set out in full in On the Union of Souls.

  • BarbeloThe first thought

    The first emanation of the Invisible Spirit: not a made thing, but the Spirit's own thought of itself, and the ground from which the fullness unfolds.

  • The PleromaThe fullness

    The whole and undivided realm of light. The world we live in is not that realm, and the difference between them is not a matter of degree.

  • The SparkWhat is yours

    Something of the fullness is held in each person. It is not earned, it cannot be granted by an institution, and no institution can take it away. Knowledge of it is what we mean by gnosis.

  • The SerpentThe revealer

    In our reading the serpent of Eden brings knowledge rather than ruin. Our predecessors were catalogued and dismissed as snake-worshippers on the strength of it. We keep the reading and decline the label.

The name

Why Barbeline

The Invisible Spirit cannot be named. That is not modesty about our vocabulary but the substance of the teaching: what lies beyond the fullness is beyond description, and a church named for the Monad would contradict itself in its own title.

Barbelo is the first thing that can be named. She is the Spirit's own thought of itself, the first emanation, the womb of the fullness — the point at which the unnameable becomes something a person can turn toward. A tradition must be named for something it can hold, and she is the first such thing there is.

The ancient writers who catalogued our predecessors called them Barbeliotae, Barbelo-worshippers. The word was theirs, not ours, and it was slightly wrong: she is venerated as the first Thought, not as the God beyond her. We take the root and leave the error. Barbeline is our own word, in our own language, for a tradition that until now was named only by the people who wished to see the back of it.

On where this layer sits among the others, and why the older labels do not fit it, see Before the Umbrella.
